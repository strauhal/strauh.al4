this archive is set in times new roman. it was commissioned for a newspaper called the times almost 100 years ago. the first versions of this archive used a modern version of helvetica but i decided to use times new roman for ubiquity and consistency since most personal computers already have a copy of it installed. another reason i chose this typeface is because of how delightfully familiar it looks. anyone who has written any paper has dealt with times new roman at one point or another. times new roman is one of the few typefaces that is instantly legible, yet invisible at the same time. stanley morison, the creator of times new roman once said "“dullness and monotony in the type-setting are far less vicious to a reader than typographical eccentricity or pleasantry.” when you're flitting through thousands of artworks on the archive, using a typeface that recedes into the background is incredibly important. i have always been tickled by the field of typography. language and words are a standardized framework of addressing concepts in the world. writing is a continuation of that framework, a tool to freeze language in metal but to also give it new abilities. typography is a further continuation and standardization of language and writing. and digital typography is yet another continuation of that
